Wix and WordPress are the two most common answers to *"how do I build a website?"*

Wix is the all-in-one website builder that made drag-and-drop famous. WordPress is the open-source platform that powers roughly 40% of the web.

They're both popular, both affordable, and both capable of building a professional-looking site. But they're built for completely different people — and choosing the wrong one wastes time, money, or both.

Here's an honest breakdown.

## The short answer

- **Wix** is right for you if you want a website live this week, don't want to deal with hosting or technical setup, and are happy paying a monthly fee.
- **WordPress** is right for you if you want full ownership of your site, need custom features, or are willing to spend a bit more time on setup in exchange for long-term flexibility.
- **Neither is universally better.** The right answer depends on what you're building and how much control you want.

## What each one actually is

**Wix** is a hosted website builder. You sign up, pick a template, and build your site in their drag-and-drop editor. Wix handles hosting, security, updates, and everything technical. You just build.

**WordPress** is a free, open-source content management system. You install it on hosting you pay for separately. Everything — themes, plugins, security, updates — is your responsibility or your developer's responsibility.

Same goal, completely different approach.

## Pricing — the real numbers

This is where most people underestimate the long-term cost difference.

### Wix

| Plan | Monthly Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light | ~$17/month | Basic site, no e-commerce |
| Core | ~$29/month | Small business features |
| Business | ~$36/month | Full e-commerce |
| Business Elite | ~$159/month | High-volume store |

Note: these are annual-billing rates. Monthly rates are 30–50% higher.

**First-year cost for a basic business site:** ~$200–$350.
**First-year cost for a store:** ~$430–$700+.

Plus: Wix charges extra for premium apps (booking systems, email marketing, etc.). Basic plans don't include everything.

### WordPress

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| WordPress software | Free |
| Hosting | $3–$30/month |
| Domain | ~$10–$15/year |
| Theme (optional) | $0–$200 one-time |
| Plugins (optional) | $0–$300/year |
| SSL | Free with most hosts |

**First-year cost for a basic business site:** ~$50–$150.
**First-year cost for a store:** ~$100–$400.

The difference is significant and compounds over time. WordPress costs less than half of Wix in most scenarios — and that gap widens every year.

## Ease of setup

**Wix wins clearly.**

You sign up, pick a template, and start dragging elements around. The editor is polished, the templates are professionally designed, and everything is handled for you. You can have a professional-looking site live in an afternoon without any technical knowledge.

**WordPress takes longer.** The setup process involves:

1. Buying hosting and a domain
2. Installing WordPress
3. Choosing and configuring a theme
4. Installing essential plugins
5. Configuring settings
6. Building pages

With experience, a few hours. Without, a week or more — especially if you hit configuration issues.

This is Wix's biggest advantage. If you want a site live this week with minimal effort, Wix is the answer.

## Design and templates

**Wix** has hundreds of professionally designed templates. They look good out of the box and are easy to customize. Fewer total options than WordPress, but consistently higher quality. You can make a Wix site look genuinely polished without design skills.

**WordPress** has thousands of themes — free and premium. You have more choice, but quality varies wildly. Some themes are beautiful, some are broken, and many look dated. Choosing the right theme for your project takes research.

For a beginner who wants a good-looking site quickly, Wix is easier.

For someone who wants complete design control, WordPress has no limits.

## The Wix editor — love it or hate it

Wix's drag-and-drop editor is genuinely clever. You can place any element anywhere. You can resize, reposition, and animate almost anything visually.

But it has a downside that catches people by surprise: **once you build a site in the Wix editor, you're locked into the Wix editor.**

If you want to move your site to WordPress later, you can't export the design. You rebuild from scratch.

WordPress themes, by contrast, are portable to some degree. If you're using a well-built WordPress theme, you can move your content and often your design to a new host without rebuilding everything.

This matters if you're making a long-term bet on your website.

## Flexibility and customization

**WordPress wins, clearly.**

Because it's open-source and runs on your own hosting, you can:

- Modify any part of the code
- Add any plugin (60,000+ in the official directory)
- Build custom features from scratch
- Integrate with any third-party service
- Change any design or layout element

Wix is customizable within limits. You can adjust colors, fonts, layouts, and add custom CSS on premium plans. But you can't fundamentally change how the platform works. Custom functionality usually requires Wix's Velo development platform (available only on higher-tier plans).

If you know you'll need custom features, WordPress will get you there. Wix might not.

## Performance

**Depends on the build, but WordPress has a higher ceiling.**

**Wix** sites are reasonably fast out of the box. The platform handles the infrastructure, so you don't need to optimize. But there's a limit to how fast Wix can be — you don't control the underlying stack.

**WordPress** performance depends entirely on your hosting and how you build the site. On good hosting with proper optimization, a WordPress site can be significantly faster than Wix. On cheap hosting with too many plugins, it can be much slower.

WordPress gives you more control over speed — and more ways to make it slow.

## E-commerce

**Wix** has built-in e-commerce (called Wix Stores). It's straightforward, has the basics you'd expect, and works fine for small stores. There are transaction fees on some plans, and the checkout flow is more limited than dedicated platforms.

**WordPress** uses WooCommerce, which is more powerful but takes more setup. WooCommerce handles everything from a small store to a large multi-vendor marketplace. It's more flexible, more customizable, and often cheaper.

For a five-product store, Wix is fine. For a store you're planning to grow, WooCommerce is the better foundation.

## Ownership

**WordPress wins, clearly.**

With WordPress, you own your site. Your hosting, your code, your data. If you want to move, you take everything with you. No platform can lock you out or change the rules.

With Wix, you rent the site. Your content lives on their servers. If you stop paying, you lose access. If they change pricing, you pay the new price. If they discontinue a feature, it's gone.

For most small businesses, this doesn't matter day-to-day. But it matters when things change — and they always do.

## Maintenance

**Wix wins.**

Wix handles everything. No updates, no security patches, no plugin conflicts. It just works.

WordPress requires ongoing attention:

- Core, theme, and plugin updates
- Security monitoring
- Backups
- Occasional troubleshooting when something breaks

For WordPress owners who don't want to deal with this, the standard answer is a maintenance service — someone to handle updates and security for a small monthly fee. It's a real cost that doesn't exist on Wix.

## Which one should you choose?

**Choose Wix if:**
- You want a beautiful site live this week
- You don't want to think about hosting, updates, or security
- You're okay with a monthly subscription
- You don't need custom features or complex functionality
- Your site is mostly about presenting your business

**Choose WordPress if:**
- You want to own your site completely
- You're on a tight budget and can't commit to monthly fees
- You need custom features, integrations, or unusual functionality
- You're building something that will grow or change significantly
- You're already familiar with WordPress or are willing to learn

**If you're genuinely unsure:** Wix is the safer first choice. It's harder to build something broken, and if you outgrow it, migrating to WordPress is a project you can plan.

## The migration trap

One thing to understand upfront: moving from Wix to WordPress is not easy.

Wix doesn't provide a clean export of your design. You can export your content (text, images, products), but the design and layout have to be rebuilt. This is a real cost if you change your mind later.

Going from WordPress to Wix is easier, but you're moving *into* a more limited platform — which rarely makes sense.

This means the decision matters. Neither is wrong, but picking the wrong one for your situation means a rebuild later.

## Bottom line

Wix is easier, faster to launch, and more polished out of the box. WordPress is cheaper long-term, more flexible, and fully yours.

Neither is the "professional" choice and neither is the "amateur" choice. They're tools for different priorities.

If you want a website and don't want to think about it, Wix.

If you want a website and want to build it your way, WordPress.
