If you're starting an online store, you've probably narrowed it down to two options: WooCommerce or Shopify.

Both are excellent. Both power millions of stores. Both can handle everything from a five-product shop to a full retail operation.

But they're built on completely different philosophies — and choosing the wrong one can cost you time, money, or flexibility down the road.

Here's an honest breakdown of how they actually compare.

## The short answer

- **Shopify** is a hosted platform. You pay a monthly fee, they host everything, and you get a polished experience with no technical setup.
- **WooCommerce** is a WordPress plugin. It's free to install, but you're responsible for hosting, maintenance, and making sure everything runs correctly.

For most small brands just starting out, **Shopify is easier**. For brands that want full ownership and no ongoing platform fees, **WooCommerce is better**. For brands on a tight budget with some technical capacity, WooCommerce wins on cost.

The rest of this post explains why.

## What each platform actually is

**Shopify** is a complete e-commerce platform you rent. You sign up, pay a monthly fee, and get a store that's hosted, maintained, and secured by Shopify. You don't touch servers, install plugins, or manage technical infrastructure.

**WooCommerce** is a free plugin that turns a WordPress site into an online store. You install WordPress (which you host somewhere), then add the WooCommerce plugin. Everything else — hosting, security, updates — is your responsibility.

Both result in a functional store. The difference is where the work happens and who's responsible for what.

## Pricing — the real numbers

This is where the two options diverge most.

### Shopify

| Plan | Monthly Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | ~$39/month | Most small stores start here |
| Shopify | ~$105/month | Better rates, more features |
| Advanced | ~$399/month | Higher volume stores |

Plus transaction fees if you don't use Shopify Payments. Plus costs for premium apps. Plus theme costs (free themes exist, premium ones run $150–$400).

**First-year cost estimate for a small store:** $500–$1,500.

### WooCommerce

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| WooCommerce plugin | Free |
| Hosting | $5–$30/month |
| Domain | ~$10–$15/year |
| SSL | Free with most hosts |
| Premium plugins (optional) | $0–$300/year |

**First-year cost estimate for a small store:** $80–$400.

The gap is significant. Over three years, Shopify costs $1,500–$4,500 while WooCommerce costs $250–$1,200 — assuming similar feature sets.

If you're a small brand testing whether e-commerce works for you, that difference matters.

## Ownership — the bigger picture

**On Shopify:** You rent the store. Your products, customers, and orders live on Shopify's servers. If you stop paying, the store stops working. You can export your data, but the platform itself is not yours.

**On WooCommerce:** You own the store. Your site, your data, your hosting account. If you want to move, you take everything with you. No platform can lock you out or change the rules on you.

For most small brands, this doesn't matter much day-to-day. But it matters a lot when:

- Shopify changes its pricing (they've done this multiple times)
- Shopify bans a product category you sell
- You want to move to a different platform
- You want custom features that Shopify doesn't support

With WooCommerce, none of these are concerns. You're in full control.

## Ease of setup

**Shopify is easier to start.** You can have a store running in an hour. The admin panel is polished, the theme setup is guided, and there's no technical configuration.

**WooCommerce takes longer.** You need to set up WordPress, install WooCommerce, configure payment gateways, choose a theme, and handle the hosting. With experience, it takes a few hours. Without, it can take a week or more.

This is the strongest argument for Shopify. If you don't want to deal with any technical setup, Shopify is the answer.

## Flexibility and customization

**WooCommerce wins, broadly.** Because it runs on WordPress, you can:

- Modify any part of the code
- Add any WordPress plugin
- Build completely custom features
- Integrate with anything that has an API
- Change any design element

Shopify is customizable too, but with real limits. You work within Shopify's ecosystem — their theme system, their apps, their rules. Custom development on Shopify requires Liquid (their templating language) and often requires their Plus plan for advanced features.

If your store needs to do something unusual, WooCommerce will let you build it. Shopify might not.

## Speed and performance

Both can be fast. Both can be slow.

**Shopify** has good baseline performance because everything is optimized for them. But adding too many apps slows it down, and you have limited control over the underlying infrastructure.

**WooCommerce** performance depends entirely on your hosting and how you build the site. A well-optimized WooCommerce store on good hosting is often *faster* than Shopify. A poorly-built one on cheap hosting is much slower.

The key: WooCommerce has a higher ceiling but also a lower floor. Shopify is more consistent but with a lower ceiling.

## Payments and checkout

**Shopify** has a built-in checkout called Shopify Payments. It's excellent — one-click setup, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Buy Now Pay Later, and a checkout flow that's been optimized for conversion across millions of stores.

If you don't use Shopify Payments, you pay extra transaction fees on top of your payment processor's fees.

**WooCommerce** integrates with any payment gateway: Stripe, PayPal, Square, Authorize.net, and dozens more. You choose the processor. There are no extra platform fees.

The WooCommerce checkout can be customized more deeply, but it requires more work to match Shopify's polish out of the box.

## Which one should you choose?

Here's a simple decision framework.

**Choose Shopify if:**
- You want a store running this week with minimal technical setup
- You don't want to handle hosting, security, or updates
- You're okay with a monthly fee
- You plan to sell primarily physical products through standard e-commerce flows
- You value polish and reliability over customizability

**Choose WooCommerce if:**
- You want to own your store completely
- You're on a tight budget and can't commit to monthly fees
- You already have a WordPress site or are comfortable with WordPress
- You need custom features that Shopify doesn't support
- You want to avoid ongoing platform dependency
- You're building something more than a basic store

**Consider both if:**
- You're not sure yet what you need — start on Shopify, migrate to WooCommerce later if you outgrow it
- You have a developer who can set up WooCommerce properly and maintain it

## The migration reality

Migrating from one platform to the other is possible, but it's painful. Products, customers, orders, and any custom setups need to be moved manually.

This means the decision matters upfront. Neither platform is "wrong" — but picking the wrong one for your situation means a migration later.

If you're genuinely unsure, **Shopify is the safer first choice** — it's harder to build something broken on Shopify, and if you outgrow it, the migration to WooCommerce is a project you can plan for.

If you know you want a long-term platform you own and control, **WooCommerce is the better foundation.**

## Bottom line

Shopify is easier and more polished. WooCommerce is cheaper and more flexible.

Small brands often assume Shopify is "the professional choice" and WooCommerce is "the budget choice." That's backwards. WooCommerce is what serious, long-term e-commerce brands often move *to* after they've outgrown hosted platforms.

If you want a store that just works, Shopify.

If you want a store you fully own, WooCommerce.

Neither is wrong. Just be clear about which one matches the kind of business you're building.
