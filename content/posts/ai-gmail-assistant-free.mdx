If you get more than a handful of emails a day, you already know the problem.

Not the "too many emails" problem — the *sorting* problem. Every morning there's a mix of:

- A genuine client question that needs a reply
- A newsletter you'll never read
- A meeting invite that needs a response
- A cold sales pitch
- A security alert
- A collaboration request
- Something that looks important but you can't tell at a glance

You end up spending the first 20 minutes of every workday just deciding what each email is. Not responding to it — just *categorising* it. By the time you've sorted through everything, your best thinking hours are gone.

This is a project I built to solve exactly that. It's a fully automated AI assistant that runs inside Gmail, sorts your inbox, applies labels, and even drafts replies for emails that need one.

It runs in the cloud. It costs nothing to operate. It doesn't need a VPS, a server, or a paid automation platform.

Here's how it works.

## What the assistant actually does

Every 10 minutes, the assistant runs quietly in the background. Here's what happens:

1. It connects to your Gmail inbox via the Gmail API
2. It pulls any unread messages
3. It sends each email to Gemini AI for classification
4. Based on the classification, it applies a Gmail label — To Respond, Collaboration, Meeting, Marketing, Cold Email, Notification, or Review Manually
5. If the email needs a reply, Gemini generates a draft response and saves it directly in Gmail
6. The email is marked as processed and won't be handled again

You don't send anything automatically. The assistant only *prepares* the drafts — you review, edit, and send them yourself.

That last point matters. Full automation of *sending* emails is risky. Full automation of *organising* them is transformative. This project sits in the safe middle.

## Why not n8n, Zapier, or Make?

The obvious way to build something like this is with an automation platform. n8n, Zapier, and Make all support Gmail triggers and AI steps.

They also come with:

- Monthly subscription costs ($20–$50/month for anything serious)
- Rate limits on the free tiers
- Data flowing through a third-party service
- Limits on how much custom logic you can write

For a personal automation like this, none of those are worth it.

Instead, the assistant runs on **GitHub Actions** — GitHub's built-in automation platform. It executes your code on a schedule, with free minutes that cover this project easily. No subscription, no rate limits that matter, no third party sitting between you and your inbox.

The tradeoff is that you have to write a bit of code. But the whole thing is designed to be beginner-friendly — you don't need deep Python knowledge, and you don't need to understand OAuth to get it running.

## The tech stack

Four pieces, all free:

**Gmail API** — Google's official API for reading emails, applying labels, and creating drafts. Requires a one-time OAuth setup, then runs on its own.

**Gemini AI** — Google's AI model. It reads each email's subject and body, decides what category it falls into, and generates draft replies when needed. Has a generous free tier.

**GitHub Actions** — Runs the Python code on a schedule. Free tier is more than enough for personal use.

**Python** — Ties everything together. Reads emails, sends them to Gemini, applies the labels and drafts based on the response.

Total ongoing cost: **$0.**

## What problems it solves

**Decision fatigue.** You stop deciding what each email is. The AI does that.

**Response latency.** Drafts are waiting for you instead of you starting from scratch. For routine enquiries, you can often just review and send.

**Missing important emails.** Instead of scanning subject lines and hoping you don't miss something, you have a labeled "To Respond" pile that tells you exactly where attention is needed.

**Inbox overwhelm.** Notifications, marketing, and cold emails get quietly filed away. You see them if you want to, but they don't clutter your main view.

## What it doesn't do (deliberately)

**It doesn't send emails.** Every draft requires your review. You stay in control.

**It doesn't read your archived emails.** Only unread messages are processed. Your history stays untouched.

**It doesn't act on emails it's unsure about.** Low-confidence classifications go to "Review Manually" instead of guessing.

**It doesn't lock you into a subscription.** It's your code, your repo, your Gmail account. If you stop using it, you just delete the GitHub repo.

## How to set it up

The setup is completely free and takes about an hour end-to-end. The main steps are:

1. **Create a GitHub repository** — where the automation lives
2. **Get a Gemini API key** — free from Google AI Studio
3. **Set up a Google Cloud project** — enables Gmail API access
4. **Generate OAuth credentials** — a one-time authorisation
5. **Store your keys in GitHub Secrets** — secure storage for your credentials
6. **Enable the GitHub Actions workflow** — the automation starts running

You don't need prior experience with Gmail APIs, OAuth, or GitHub Actions. The workflow is designed to be configurable for anyone who can follow instructions and copy-paste.

Every label is created automatically during the first run — you don't need to set up Gmail labels manually.

## Customising it for your needs

The assistant is meant to be a starting point. Once it's running, you can:

- **Add your own labels** — Client, Support, Invoice, Urgent, whatever fits your workflow
- **Change the classification rules** — make it stricter, add categories, adjust thresholds
- **Change the draft style** — formal, casual, friendly, technical — it's a prompt you can edit
- **Adjust the frequency** — every 5, 10, 15, or 30 minutes
- **Expand it** — daily email summaries, Slack notifications, priority scoring, CRM integration

Because the code is yours, every part of it is editable.
